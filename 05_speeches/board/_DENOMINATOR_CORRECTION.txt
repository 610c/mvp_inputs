WHAT WAS WRONG
An earlier count used the Board SPEECHES INDEX (/newsevents/speech/2026-speeches.htm) as the
denominator and reported 13 in-window Board appearances, with Warsh at ONE for the whole year.
That was wrong. The speeches index lists only events the Board publishes as a "Speech" with a
prepared text. The MONTHLY CALENDAR PAGES (item 20) list a second category - "Discussion" -
which the index omits entirely. These are real public appearances by governors, on the record,
often on monetary policy, with no prepared text and therefore no index entry.

WHAT THIS CHANGES
1. WARSH MADE TWO IN-WINDOW APPEARANCES, NOT ONE. The 1 July ECB Sintra policy panel is a
   Chairman-level monetary-policy appearance six weeks before Jackson Hole. The earlier
   claim that he "gave exactly one speech all year" was an artefact of the index, not a fact
   about his behaviour.
2. THE INDEX SYSTEMATICALLY UNDERCOUNTS, AND NOT AT RANDOM. It drops exactly the format that
   produces no prepared text. So a text-based corpus and an appearance-based denominator
   diverge, and they diverge MOST for participants who favour conversational formats.
   This is the same bias already documented for Goolsbee at the Chicago Fed, where all
   in-window appearances are fireside chats with no transcript.
3. THE CORRECT DENOMINATOR IS THE CALENDAR, NOT THE SPEECH INDEX. This is precisely what
   item 20 was for. Any salience or volume measure should be built on the monthly calendar
   pages (12_calendars/) and treat the speech index as the *numerator* - documents available -
   rather than as the universe of appearances.

THE POINT ABOUT TOPIC FILTERING, RESTATED WITH A CONCRETE CASE
The 2026-09-01 Barr speech is titled "Unlocking Opportunities for Workers and
Entrepreneurs with a Criminal Record" and is tagged to financial inclusion.
Any pipeline that selects speeches by monetary-policy topic DISCARDS IT. Yet
it contains the single most explicit pre-September policy commitment in the
whole window:

  "if inflation appears not to be moderating sufficiently, then I think we
   should act decisively to raise rates."

This is exactly what the instruction "every speech, not only monetary policy
ones" was protecting against. The denominator argument and the numerator
argument turn out to be the same argument: filter by topic and you lose both
the count AND the content.
