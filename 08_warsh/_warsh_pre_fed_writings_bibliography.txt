ACCESS NOTE — WHAT COULD NOT BE RETRIEVED AND WHY
================================================================
1. THE WSJ OP-EDS ARE PAYWALLED. Every WSJ item above carries Hoover's
   "[Subscription Required]" flag. These cannot be fetched. This is a
   genuine, permanent gap in the corpus unless a subscription is supplied.
   The BIBLIOGRAPHY is still analytically useful on its own:
     - It is a dated series of ~35 op-eds spanning 2010–2025, which is a
       usable proxy for Warsh's public salience and its timing.
     - The titles alone track a consistent thesis: Fed mission creep, Fed
       overreach, inflation as a Fed choice, supply-side growth. Compare
       these titles with the confirmation statement's "the Fed must stay in
       its lane" and with the Jackson Hole address.
     - Note the cadence: roughly one op-ed a quarter from 2010 to 2016,
       thinning through 2017–2021, then re-accelerating sharply in 2024–2025
       as the chairmanship came into view (Jul 2024, Jan 2025, Apr 2025,
       Nov 2025). That acceleration is itself a signal.

2. NINE DATES ARE MARKED [date not captured]. These are page-boundary
   artifacts: the Hoover listing renders the last item of each page with its
   date below the pagination control, and the date was clipped. The TITLES
   are exact. I have NOT guessed at the dates rather than record a wrong one.

3. "MONEY MATTERS" (2022, AEI chapter) — named in the corpus request and
   cited at footnote 13 of the Jackson Hole address — does NOT appear on the
   Hoover index at all. Hoover indexes Hoover-affiliated output; an AEI
   volume chapter falls outside it. Needs to be sourced from AEI directly.
   NOT YET RETRIEVED.

4. The 2014 BOE MPC transparency review IS retrievable and is captured
   separately in this directory.
